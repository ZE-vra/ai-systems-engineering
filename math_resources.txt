1. Your mathematics learning path

There are six main areas we'll work through over the next several years.

Stage	Mathematics	Why you need it

1	Algebra and functions	Build confidence manipulating equations and understanding relationships.
2	Logic and discrete mathematics	Understand algorithms, proofs, sets, graphs, and computational reasoning.
3	Probability and statistics	Understand uncertainty, data, distributions, and model evaluation.
4	Linear algebra	Understand vectors, matrices, transformations, and neural network computations.
5	Calculus	Understand derivatives, gradients, and how machine learning models are optimized.
6	Optimization and advanced probability	Develop deeper understanding of model training, statistical learning, and research.


You may already understand some of these subjects from school. We should review your existing knowledge rather than make you study everything from scratch.

This is a well-established university textbook.

Study:

Sets and functions

Mathematical reasoning and proofs

Algorithms and complexity

Counting

Graphs and trees

Discrete probability


This book is optional to purchase immediately. First, we can use free material and decide later whether owning the textbook would benefit you.

Book 3: Linear algebra

Linear algebra is one of the most important mathematical subjects for your eventual AI specialization.

It explains the mathematics of vectors, matrices, linear transformations, eigenvalues, and related concepts.

These ideas appear throughout machine learning and deep learning.

The course by Professor Gilbert Strang includes lectures, notes, exercises, exams, and solutions. 

What to study:

1. Vectors and systems of linear equations.


2. Matrix operations and elimination.


3. Vector spaces and linear independence.


4. Basis and dimension.


5. Orthogonality and projections.


6. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors.


7. Least-squares problems.


8. Singular value decomposition.



You don't need to begin this course right now. We'll build the necessary algebra foundation first.

4. How to study mathematics without getting overwhelmed

Here's the approach I'd recommend.

Suppose you're studying functions.

Step 1: Read the textbook.

Learn what a function is, study the worked examples, and identify any unfamiliar notation.

Step 2: Watch a lesson if necessary.

Use a course such as Khan Academy if the textbook explanation isn't clear.

Step 3: Solve problems yourself.

Work through exercises without immediately looking at the answers.

Step 4: Investigate mistakes.

If you get an answer wrong, identify whether the problem was algebra, misunderstanding the concept, or making a calculation error.

Step 5: Explain the concept.

Try explaining the idea in your own words and give an example.

Step 6: Apply it to programming when appropriate.

For instance, after learning about functions and graphs, you could write a small Python program to calculate and display values of a mathematical function.

This helps connect mathematical understanding with your software engineering work.

5. How much time should you dedicate to mathematics?

Given your university coursework, job, and independent software engineering studies, I wouldn't recommend trying to study mathematics for several hours every day.

Start with 30 minutes per study day, four or five days a week.

For example:

Day	Mathematics activity

Monday	Read and understand a new concept.
Tuesday	Solve practice problems.
Wednesday	Continue the lesson and review examples.
Thursday	Solve problems independently.
Friday	Review mistakes and test your understanding.


That's two to two and a half hours each week.

As the mathematics becomes more demanding, you can increase the time when your schedule permits.

The important thing is to study consistently without allowing mathematics to consume all the time you need for programming and building projects.
